# The physical and structural properties of superconducting A15-type Nb-Sn alloys

**Devantay, Jorda, Decroux, Muller, Flükiger (1981)** — Journal of Materials Science 16, 2145–2153

DOI: 10.1007/BF00542375

*Zotero collections: Superconducting Materials > Nb3Sn; Reference & Background > Reviews*

---

## Summary

Systematic study of bulk A15 [[Nb3Sn]] prepared over the full composition range (18–25 at% Sn) using arc-melting + annealing, characterizing Tc, lattice parameter, and structural order as functions of Sn content. The central result is a **linear Tc vs Sn composition** relationship: Tc rises from ~6 K at 18.5 at% Sn to ~18 K at 25 at% Sn (slope ≈ 1.85 K/at% Sn). A critical finding for thin-film work is that **long-range ordering (LRO) disorder contributes <0.5 K** to Tc suppression — Sn deficiency, not LRO, is the primary suppressor of Tc in Nb3Sn.

---

## Key Findings

- **Tc is linear in Sn content** across the A15 homogeneity range:
  - 18.5 at% Sn → Tc ≈ 6.0 K
  - 20 at% Sn → Tc ≈ 9 K
  - 22 at% Sn → Tc ≈ 12 K
  - 24 at% Sn → Tc ≈ 15–16 K
  - 25 at% Sn (stoichiometric) → Tc ≈ 18.0–18.3 K
  - Slope: ~1.85 K/at% Sn
- **LRO disorder is secondary**: Quenched samples (disordered A15) vs furnace-cooled (well-ordered A15) at the same composition differ by <0.5 K in Tc → long-range crystallographic order matters much less than stoichiometry
- **Lattice parameter linear in Sn**: a increases monotonically with Sn content; provides a structural fingerprint for composition determination from XRD
- **Tetragonal distortion** (cubic → tetragonal martensitic transformation) occurs at ~24.5 at% Sn during cooling; broadens superconducting transition width; not a composition effect on Tc but a structural effect
- **A15 homogeneity range** confirmed: 18–25 at% Sn; below 18 at% the A15 phase decomposes; above 25 at% other phases (NbSn2 etc.) appear
- **Resistivity**: Normal-state resistivity ρ increases with Sn deficiency (more Nb anti-site disorder → stronger scattering)

## Physical Mechanism

Sn content controls Tc through the **electronic density of states (DOS)**:
- In perfect A15, Nb chains provide a sharp DOS peak near the Fermi level (Van Hove singularity → strong electron–phonon coupling → high Tc)
- Sn deficiency → excess Nb occupies Sn sites (Nb anti-site disorder) → disrupts Nb chain continuity → smears DOS peak → reduces coupling → lower Tc
- This is fundamentally different from LRO disorder (random swaps of Nb and Sn on their own sublattices), which has minimal impact on the chain structure
- Therefore: **the dominant path to high Tc is achieving 25 at% Sn, not achieving LRO perfection**

---

## Methods Used

- Arc-melting of Nb + Sn in controlled stoichiometries across 18–25 at% Sn
- Annealing at 800–1100°C for homogenization
- XRD (Cu Kα) — lattice parameter measurement; Rietveld analysis
- Resistivity (four-probe) — normal state and Tc measurement
- Some samples quenched (disordered) vs slow-cooled (ordered) to isolate LRO effect

## Materials Studied

- [[Nb3Sn]] — A15 phase across full homogeneity range; primary subject
- Binary Nb-Sn system; no ternary additions

## Open Questions

- Does the Tc-composition linear relationship hold identically for thin-film Nb3Sn (where grain boundary Sn segregation creates inhomogeneity), or is it a bulk-only result?
- What is the minimum spatial scale of Sn inhomogeneity that measurably suppresses the global Tc?
- Can strain (as in Kampwirth effect) be disentangled from composition effects in Cu-substrate films?

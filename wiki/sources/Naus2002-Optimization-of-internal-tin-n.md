# Optimization of internal-tin niobium-tin composites

**Naus (2002)**

*Zotero collections: Superconducting Materials > Nb3Sn*

---

## Abstract

The heat treatment of internal-Sn wires has two functions: (1) to mix the interfilamentary Cu with Sn from the core, and (2) to react the Sri with Nb to form the superconducting Nb-Sn A15 phase. To study the effectiveness of CU-Sn mixing, the Cu-Sn microstructures were examined in two very different internal-Sn composites after heat treatments of 24 and 150 hours at each of 10 different temperatures (up to 600°C). None of the heat treatments generated a single-phase Cu-Sn microstructure. It was concluded that complete mixing of the Cu and Sn is highly unlikely in commercial wires. However, it was found that Cu-Sn microstructure inhomogeneity has no effect on the critical temperature (Tc), irreversibility field (H*) or inductively measured critical current density (Jc) of fully reacted Nb3Sn wire. Thus control of the superconducting properties lies with the composite design and the A15 reaction heat treatment. However, a Cu-Nb-Sn ternary intermetallic was discovered, which forms as a result of dissolution of Nb and therefore has deleterious effects on Jc. As the Cu fraction within the filament bundle decreases, lengthy Cu-Sn mixing heat treatments must be employed to limit formation of this ternary phase. A direct correlation was found between T c and H*Kramer that is strongly dependent on the Sn concentration in the A15 layer, emphasizing the need to provide proper Sn stoichiometry in the Cu-Nb-Sn package and to effectively contain the Sn within the bundle. Commonly used Nb diffusion barriers can react completely through locally, resulting in Sn loss from the A15 layer and degradation of the superconducting properties. To generate non-Cu Jc(12 T, 4.2 K) = 3000 A/mm2, the required Jc within the A15 layer is 4610 A/mm2, equal to the best literature values. In the best performing internal-Sn conductor to date, it is shown that non-Cu Jc(12 T, 4.2 K) of 3000 A/mm2 should be produced by replacing 5 vol.% of the Sn core with Nb or by replacing 3 vol.% of interfilamentary Cu with Nb and Sn.

## Summary

PhD thesis (Wisconsin ASC, Larbalestier/Lee group) on internal-tin Nb3Sn wire optimization. **The landmark result: discovery of the Cu–Nb–Sn ternary intermetallic later named "[[Nausite]]"** — a phase that forms by *dissolving the Nb filaments* and is deleterious to Jc. Also: Cu–Sn mixing inhomogeneity (long feared) turns out *not* to affect Tc/H\*/Jc of fully-reacted wire — what controls superconducting properties is composite design + the A15 reaction heat treatment + keeping the Sn properly contained and stoichiometric in the bundle.

## Key Findings

### 1. Discovery of the Cu–Nb–Sn ternary (= "Nausite") ★
- A previously unreported **Cu–Nb–Sn ternary intermetallic**, composition **~Cu–22 at% Nb–61 at% Sn** (later refined to **(Nb₀.₇₅Cu₀.₂₅)Sn₂**).
- **Forms only when a non-α (Sn-rich) Cu–Sn phase contacts Nb at T < ~400 °C.** Single-phase below ~401 °C, multiphase above ~428 °C.
- **Grows by dissolving the Nb filaments** → reduces the longitudinally continuous Nb cross-section → directly lowers Jc,non-Cu.
- **Even after it converts to A15, it forms disconnected nodules** that cannot pass supercurrent along the wire length. Confirmed deleterious in a low-Cu / high-Sn composite.
- ⇒ A serious obstacle to raising Jc by simply increasing the Sn:Cu ratio in the bundle. As Cu fraction drops, **longer Cu–Sn mixing heat treatments are needed to limit ternary formation.**

### 2. Cu–Sn mixing homogeneity is a red herring for final properties
- No heat treatment (24 or 150 h, 10 temperatures up to 600 °C) produced a single-phase Cu–Sn microstructure; **full Cu–Sn mixing is essentially unachievable in commercial wire.**
- But Cu–Sn inhomogeneity has **no effect on Tc, H\*, or inductive Jc of fully-reacted wire.** Control lies elsewhere (design + A15 reaction).
- The 200 °C step's real function is likely to **encase the Sn core in higher-melting Cu–Sn phases to prevent Sn-burst**, not to "mix."

### 3. Stoichiometry and Sn containment govern Jc
- Strong Tc ↔ H\*_Kramer correlation **dependent on Sn concentration in the A15** → must deliver proper Sn stoichiometry and contain Sn in the bundle.
- **Nb diffusion barriers can react through locally** → Sn leaks out → A15 Sn loss and property degradation.
- Grain size is the key Jc lever (GBs = pinning centers): lower reaction T → finer grains but needs higher Sn; Ti/Ta/Mg inhibit grain growth. Target: A15-layer Jc(12 T, 4.2 K) = 4610 A/mm² for non-Cu Jc = 3000 A/mm².

## Methods Used

- Internal-tin composite wires (two contrasting designs), systematic heat-treatment matrix (24/150 h × 10 temperatures ≤ 600 °C).
- SEM-BSE + EDS for Cu–Sn and Cu–Nb–Sn phase identification; direct powder synthesis (Cu+Sn+Nb pellet, 380 °C) to reproduce the ternary.
- Tc (inductive), H\* / Kramer extrapolation, inductive Jc.

## Open Questions / relevance to FSU

- Nausite is an **internal-tin/RRP wire** phenomenon (Sn-rich liquid contacting Nb). Does an analog form in **FSU bronze films** where the Sn is delivered through a Cu–Sn layer? The trigger condition (non-α Cu–Sn touching Nb < 400 °C) is a useful diagnostic to watch.
- For SRF (FSU's goal) the Jc/grain-size logic is secondary — but **Nb dissolution and disconnected/coarse A15 still matter for surface quality and Sn uniformity.**

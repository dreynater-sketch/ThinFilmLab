# Rapid Nb3Sn film growth by sputtering Nb on hot bronze

**Withanage, Juliao, Cooley (2021)** — Superconductor Science and Technology | NHMFL / Florida State University

DOI: 10.1088/1361-6668/abf66f

---

## Summary

FSU/NHMFL study comparing two routes for forming [[Nb3Sn]] thin films on Cu-15wt%Sn bronze substrates using [[DC Magnetron Sputtering]] of Nb: (1) hot-bronze route — sputtering Nb directly onto bronze held at >700°C — and (2) low-temperature deposition followed by post-reaction anneal at the same temperatures. The hot-bronze route achieves growth rates of 33 nm/min (10× faster than post-reaction route) by enabling simultaneous Sn diffusion and A15 phase formation during deposition. Both routes yield fully coalesced films with Tc 14–16 K — well below bulk 18.3 K — attributed primarily to CTE mismatch elastic strain between Nb3Sn and bronze. The hot-bronze route produces columnar (Thornton zone 2) grain structure while post-reaction gives equiaxed grains similar to reacted Nb3Sn wire.

---

## Key Findings

- **Growth rate 33 nm/min (hot bronze)** — 10× faster than post-reaction at same temperature; enables thick film formation without extended annealing
- **Tc 14–16 K** on both routes — 2–4 K below bulk 18.3 K; attributed to CTE mismatch elastic strain (Kampwirth effect) from bronze substrate
- **Sn content**: ~26.3 at% in hot-bronze films vs ~24.5 at% by post-reaction — both in A15 stability window; hot-bronze route gives Sn-richer films
- **Lattice parameter** consistent with measured Sn content and predicted elastic strain for both routes — confirms strain origin of Tc suppression
- **Grain structure**: hot-bronze → columnar zone 2 (Thornton); post-reaction → equiaxed, resembles Nb3Sn wire microstructure
- **Cu contamination**: ~0.9 at% in hot-bronze films vs ~3.3 at% in post-reaction — hot-bronze slightly less Cu incorporation despite continuous high-T exposure
- **Surface roughness**: Ra 7–15 nm over 100 μm² scans — both routes fully coalesced films, <20 nm Ra
- **Sample matrix**: H1 (650°C hot-bronze), H2 (715°C hot-bronze), PR1 (650°C post-react), PR2 (715°C post-react)

## Copper/Bronze Substrate Constraints

1. **CTE mismatch** → elastic strain frozen in on cooling → Tc suppression below 18.3 K (Kampwirth effect)
2. **Cu interdiffusion** — Cu incorporates into film; post-reaction route shows more Cu contamination (~3.3%) than hot-bronze (~0.9%)
3. **Sn supply** — bronze provides Sn reservoir; hot-bronze route enables continuous Sn supply during film formation
4. **Lattice strain** — in-plane mismatch between Nb3Sn and bronze substrate shifts Tc

## Methods Used

- [[DC Magnetron Sputtering]] — Nb target on Cu-15wt%Sn bronze substrates; two routes: (1) substrate held at >700°C during deposition (hot bronze), (2) low-T deposition + post-anneal at same temperature
- Bronze substrate prep and temperature monitoring via thermocouples on substrate holder
- Substrates: Cu-15wt%Sn bronze (commercial bronze alloy)

## Characterization Techniques

- [[XRD]] — A15 phase identification; lattice parameter extraction; strain analysis
- [[SEM]] — surface morphology; grain structure imaging; cross-section analysis
- [[EDX/EDS]] — elemental quantification of Sn, Cu content in film
- **VSM or SQUID** — Tc measurement from diamagnetic transition; transition width

## Materials Studied

- [[Nb3Sn]] — A15 superconductor; target phase formed from Nb + Sn
- [[Bronze]] — Cu-15wt%Sn; Sn source and substrate; introduces CTE mismatch and Cu contamination
- [[Niobium]] — sputtering target material

## Open Questions

- Can the hot-bronze route be applied to real elliptical cavity geometry? (curved surface conformality issue)
- Does the columnar grain structure (zone 2) degrade or improve RF performance vs equiaxed grains?
- Can elastic strain be reduced by graded-CTE interlayer to recover Tc toward 18.3 K?
- What is the RF performance (Q0, Eacc) of hot-bronze films in actual SRF cavity tests?
- Can deposition temperature be optimized to balance grain size, Sn content, and CTE strain effects?

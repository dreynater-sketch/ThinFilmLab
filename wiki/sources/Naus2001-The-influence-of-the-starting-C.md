# Naus, Lee, Larbalestier 2001 — Cu-Sn starting phase and A15 properties

Note: PDF filename is `naus2002.pdf` but paper was published March 2001 (ASC 2000 proceedings issue). Distinct from Naus2000 (interdiffusion paper) and Naus2002 (PhD thesis); this is a third Naus paper.

## Summary

High-Sn internal-Sn Nb3Sn wire (Cu-29 at.% Sn overall) given five different mixing heat treatments (217–604°C) before identical A15 reaction (180h at 650°C). Result: the Cu-Sn phase mix going into the A15 reaction has **no significant effect** on T_c, H*, H_c2, or J_c (Δm) of the reacted wire. Practical implication: the costly Sn-homogenization step in internal-Sn wire HT can be substantially shortened.

## Key Findings

- **T_c independent of mixing HT** (Fig. 3): five wires homogenized at 362, 402, 428, 510, 533°C all show identical SQUID-measured T_c traces, mid-point ~15.5 K.
- **Multiple Cu-Sn phases coexist** even after the highest-temperature mixing (Table I). δ-phase absent above 350°C — α and ε predominate.
- **Cu-Nb-Sn ternary phase grows into Nb core** during mixing HT (Fig. 2). Single phase at HT ≤ 402°C, multi-phase at ≥ 426°C. Composition: Cu-23at%Nb-62at%Sn surrounded by Cu-20at%Nb-30at%Sn. *Direct evidence of Nb consumption / dissolution by Cu-Sn matrix at moderate temperatures.*
- **Mg-Cu-Sn ternary particles** in conductor core (jelly-roll, Mg-doped Sn) — unreacted.
- **Sn liquefaction → diffusion-barrier failure** identified anecdotally for long wires — excessive hydrostatic pressure from liquid Sn weakens barrier (Section IV).

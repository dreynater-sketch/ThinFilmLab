# Nb3Sn Superconducting Radiofrequency Cavities: a Maturing Technology

**Posen, Liepe, Eremeev, Pudasaini, Reece (2022)** — Snowmass 2021 White Paper | Fermilab / Cornell / JLab

---

## Summary

Community white paper advocating increased investment in [[Nb3Sn]] [[SRF Cavities]] R&D. Summarizes field state ca. 2022: best cavities reach ~24 MV/m at Q0 >10^10 at 4.4 K; progress has been rapid (5→24 MV/m over 1990s–2020); gradient is limited by surface defects rather than fundamental material limits. Argues Nb3Sn's Tc ~18 K enables 4.4 K operation (vs 2 K for Nb), reducing cryogenic costs by ~10×, and its superheating field predicts a theoretical maximum of ~100 MV/m.

---

## Key Findings

- **Tc ~18 K** — ~2× niobium (9.2 K); enables Q0 >10^10 at 4.4 K instead of 2 K
- **Cryogenic efficiency**: 4.4 K operation reduces cryogenic plant power/cost by ~10× vs 2 K Nb operation
- **Best gradient** (2022): ~24 MV/m at Q0 >10^10 at 4.4 K; niobium reaches ~50 MV/m
- **Theoretical max**: superheating field corresponds to ~100 MV/m — 2× niobium's limit
- **Coherence length ~3–4 nm** — ~10× shorter than Nb; makes RF surface highly sensitive to surface defects
- **Progress trajectory**: ~5 MV/m (1990s) → ~13 MV/m (2014) → ~18 MV/m (2015) → ~24 MV/m (2020)
- **Gradient limitation**: surface defects, not intrinsic material; improved surface preparation expected to push gradient higher
- **Axion haloscope**: Q0 ~5×10^5 demonstrated at 6 T and 4.2 K — large improvement over copper for dark sector searches
- **Active R&D groups (2022)**: Cornell, JLab, Fermilab; CERN, STFC, NHMFL/**FSU**/UT-Arlington, IMP, ULVAC/KEK, Peking U, ODU, Ultramet

## Applications Discussed

- **High duty-factor HEP**: FCC-ee, CePC e+e- circular colliders; DUNE/PIP-II neutrino driver
- **High-gradient linacs**: ILC energy upgrade (multi-TeV)
- **Axion haloscopes**: high Q0 in multi-tesla magnetic fields → improved scan rate vs copper cavities
- **Compact/cryocooler-cooled**: no liquid He plant; isotope production, UED, wastewater treatment, industrial linacs

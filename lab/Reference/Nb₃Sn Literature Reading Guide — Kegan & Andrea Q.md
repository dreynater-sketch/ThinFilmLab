# 🧪 Paper 1 — Lu et al. 2025

**Citation:** Ming Lu et al., *Phys. Scr.* **100**, 085996 (2025)

**Title:** The impact of Cu/Sn precursor tin content on the properties and growth mechanism of Nb₃Sn thin films via bronze methods

## 👤 Kegan — Characterization Focus

- **Phase identification (XRD):** Track how the relative intensity of Nb₃Sn, Cu₃Sn, and Cu₆Sn₅ peaks evolves as Cu/Sn precursor Sn content is varied. Pay attention to which phases appear at low vs. high Sn concentrations.
- **Grain morphology (SEM):** Note grain size distributions reported across the Sn-content series. How does grain size relate to Sn availability during reaction?
- **Stoichiometry mapping (EDS/RBS):** Identify how Sn at% in the final film varies with precursor composition. What is the window for achieving 25–26 at% Sn (stoichiometric Nb₃Sn)?
- **Superconducting transitions (Tc, ΔTc):** Record reported Tc onset and transition width for each precursor condition. Sharp transitions (ΔTc < 1K) indicate phase homogeneity — flag any conditions that achieve this.
- **Key figure to anchor:** Any plot showing Tc or XRD peak ratio vs. Sn content in the Cu/Sn precursor layer.

## ⚙️ Andrea Q — Deposition Focus

- **Precursor stack architecture:** Describe the layer sequence (e.g., Nb / Cu-Sn bilayer, or Nb / Cu / Sn trilayer). What are the nominal thicknesses and Sn:Cu ratios tested?
- **Deposition method:** Is this magnetron sputtering, thermal evaporation, or co-deposition? What base pressure and substrate conditions are reported?
- **Reaction temperature and atmosphere:** What anneal temperatures, ramp rates, and atmospheres (vacuum, Ar, forming gas) are used for the bronze reaction?
- **Substrate compatibility:** Is this done on Cu, Nb, or both? Note any adhesion issues or delamination observations.
- **Growth mechanism discussion:** What mechanistic model does the paper propose for how Sn diffuses from the Cu-Sn reservoir into the Nb layer? Compare to Andre's "Sn-reservoir design" concept.
- **Deposition parameter to benchmark:** Record the specific Cu:Sn ratio (e.g., wt% or at%) that produces the best-performing film.

# 🧪 Paper 2 — Lu et al. 2026

**Citation:** Ming Lu et al., *Supercond. Sci. Technol.* **39**, 035005 (2026)

**Title:** Preparation of the Cu-based Nb₃Sn sample via bronze route for quadrupole resonator testing

## 👤 Kegan — Characterization Focus

- **RF performance (Q factor):** Note the measured quality factors reported from quadrupole resonator testing. What frequency and temperature are used? How does Q compare to pure Nb baseline?
- **Surface resistance (Rs):** If BCS and residual resistance components are separated, record both. Rs directly ties to grain boundary density and impurity levels.
- **Microstructural characterization post-RF test:** Does the paper report SEM or XRD of samples before vs. after RF exposure? Look for evidence of tin redistribution or phase changes under RF fields.
- **Tc and RRR (residual resistivity ratio):** Record any DC superconducting measurements used to screen samples before RF testing.
- **Key figure to anchor:** Q₀ vs. temperature or Q₀ vs. accelerating gradient curve.

## ⚙️ Andrea Q — Deposition Focus

- **Scale-up from coupons to cavity geometry:** How does the deposition process change when coating a curved cavity geometry vs. flat coupon? What fixtures or tooling are described?
- **Film thickness control:** What thickness is targeted for Cu-Sn and Nb layers? How is thickness verified (profilometry, RBS, quartz crystal monitor)?
- **Reaction heat treatment on Cu substrate:** What is the maximum temperature, and how is oxidation of the Cu substrate managed during anneal?
- **Sn-reservoir approach:** Does the paper explicitly use a reservoir layer strategy? If so, compare the layer architecture to Paper 1 — is it the same or modified?
- **Process reproducibility:** Are multiple cavity runs reported? What is the run-to-run variation in key parameters?

# 🧪 Paper 3 — Viklund et al. 2026 (Fermilab)

**Citation:** Eric Viklund, David N. Seidman, Sam Posen, *Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory* preprint (March 12, 2026)

**Title:** Microstructural Characterization of Nb₃Sn Thin Films Using FIB Tomography

## 👤 Kegan — Characterization Focus

- **FIB tomography methodology:** Understand the focused ion beam serial sectioning workflow. What slice thickness and volume are imaged? This is a 3D microstructure technique — describe the reconstruction.
- **Grain boundary network:** What is the 3D grain size distribution? Are grains equiaxed or columnar? How does grain aspect ratio affect RF surface resistance?
- **Void and defect distribution:** FIB tomography can reveal subsurface voids, tin-poor patches, and interface defects invisible to surface SEM. Note what defect types are identified and their density.
- **Interface characterization (Nb₃Sn / Nb or Nb₃Sn / Cu):** What is the interface roughness and composition? Is there an interphase layer?
- **Comparison to 2D SEM:** Note any cases where 3D tomography reveals features that 2D cross-section SEM would miss. This is critical for understanding why some films with good surface SEM still have poor RF performance.
- **Key figure to anchor:** 3D reconstructed volume showing grain boundaries and/or void distribution.

## ⚙️ Andrea Q — Deposition Focus

- **Sample origin (Sn vapor vs. sputtering):** What deposition method produced the films being tomographed? Are the films representative of standard Fermilab Nb₃Sn processing (Sn vapor diffusion on Nb cavities)?
- **How deposition parameters correlate to observed microstructure:** Do the authors connect specific deposition choices (substrate temp, Sn flux) to observed grain morphology or defect density?
- **Implications for your deposition approach:** If columnar grains are linked to poor RF performance, does this implicate any of Andre's sputtering conditions? Note this for group discussion.
- **Film thickness and uniformity:** What film thickness range is examined? How uniform is the Nb₃Sn layer thickness across the tomography volume?

# 🧪 Paper 4 — Fonnesu et al.

**Citation:** D. Fonnesu et al. (INFN Legnaro / HZB collaboration)

**Title:** Recipe optimization and SRF test of Cu-compatible Nb₃Sn films by DC magnetron sputtering from a stoichiometric target

## 👤 Kegan — Characterization Focus

- **Rs vs. temperature:** This is an SRF paper — the primary performance metric is surface resistance. Record BCS resistance, residual resistance, and the temperature dependence.
- **XRD phase purity:** Does the stoichiometric target approach produce single-phase Nb₃Sn or mixed phases? Compare peak widths (FWHM) to papers using multilayer approaches.
- **SEM grain size and morphology:** The stoichiometric target should produce a different grain structure than a bronze-route diffusion process. Quantify the difference if data is provided.
- **Tc and ΔTc from DC measurements:** Benchmark against Andre's ΔTc < 1K result. Is the transition width comparable?
- **SRF cavity Q₀:** What Q₀ is reported and at what temperature and field? This is the direct application benchmark.

## ⚙️ Andrea Q — Deposition Focus

- **Stoichiometric target composition:** A Nb₃Sn compound target is used — understand the challenges of maintaining stoichiometry under sputtering (preferential sputtering of Sn). How does the paper address this?
- **DC vs. RF magnetron sputtering:** Why DC? What power densities and pressures are used? What is the deposition rate?
- **Substrate heating during deposition:** Is the substrate heated in-situ or is a post-deposition anneal used? This directly determines grain size and phase formation temperature.
- **Cu substrate adhesion and diffusion barrier:** Does the paper use a diffusion barrier (Ta, Nb, Mo) between Cu and Nb₃Sn? Compare to Andre's diffusion barrier studies.
- **Post-deposition processing:** Is any annealing step applied after sputtering? What temperature and duration?
- **Scalability to cavity geometry:** This group targets Cu-compatible SRF cavities — how do they adapt planar sputtering to cavity interiors?

# 🧪 Paper 5 — Valizadeh et al. (Daresbury / Cockcroft)

**Citation:** R. Valizadeh et al., ASTeC, STFC Daresbury Laboratory

**Title:** First 1.3 GHz Nb Cavity Coated with Nb₃Sn Thin Film Deposition by PVD Magnetron Sputtering

## 👤 Kegan — Characterization Focus

- **First-ever PVD-coated 1.3 GHz Nb cavity:** Appreciate the significance — this is a landmark result. What Q₀ and accelerating gradient are reported?
- **Comparison to Sn vapor diffusion baseline:** The standard Nb₃Sn cavity process uses Sn vapor diffusion at ~1100°C. How does PVD magnetron sputtering compare in terms of Rs and Q₀?
- **Surface and cross-section characterization:** What SEM, XRD, or EDS data are reported for the coated cavity surface? Are there grain size or phase purity data?
- **Tc of coated surface:** Is a local Tc measurement reported (e.g., via RF or DC proxy)? Does the film show the full Tc ~18K of bulk Nb₃Sn?
- **Key figure to anchor:** Q₀ vs. E_acc (accelerating gradient) or Q₀ vs. temperature.

## ⚙️ Andrea Q — Deposition Focus

- **Coating geometry for a full 1.3 GHz cavity:** How do you sputter coat the interior of an elliptical 1.3 GHz cavity? Describe the fixture, target placement, and any rotation or scanning strategy.
- **Nb substrate vs. Cu substrate:** This paper uses Nb cavities (not Cu). Note the implications — no diffusion barrier needed, higher reaction temperature possible. How does this differ from the Cu-compatible approach?
- **PVD target and power parameters:** What target material, power, Ar pressure, and substrate-to-target distance are used?
- **In-situ vs. ex-situ reaction:** Is the Nb₃Sn phase formed during deposition (heated substrate) or in a separate anneal? What temperatures are involved?
- **Film uniformity challenge:** Coating a 3D cavity uniformly is a major challenge. Does the paper report thickness uniformity measurements? What gradient is acceptable?

# 🧪 Paper 6 — Sayeed et al. — Substrate Temperature (ODU/Jefferson Lab)

**Citation:** Md Nizam Sayeed et al., Old Dominion University / Jefferson Lab

**Title:** Effect of substrate temperature on the growth of Nb₃Sn film on Nb by multilayer sputtering

## 👤 Kegan — Characterization Focus

- **Substrate temperature series:** This paper directly varies substrate temperature during deposition. Map out the Tc, XRD peak intensity, and grain size as a function of temperature — this is the core dataset.
- **XRD crystallinity vs. temperature:** At what substrate temperature does the A15 Nb₃Sn phase first appear? Where does it optimize? Note the FWHM of the (210) or dominant Nb₃Sn peak.
- **SEM grain size quantification:** Is there a systematic grain size vs. temperature relationship? Compare to Andre's grain sizes (200–500 nm).
- **RBS depth profiling:** If RBS is used, note the Nb/Sn ratio as a function of depth. Does the composition gradient change with substrate temperature?
- **Tc and ΔTc benchmark:** Record the best transition sharpness achieved and at what temperature condition.

## ⚙️ Andrea Q — Deposition Focus

- **Multilayer stack design:** What is the Nb/Sn or Nb/Cu-Sn layer sequence? How many alternating layers? What individual layer thicknesses?
- **Substrate heating hardware:** How is the substrate heated during sputtering? What temperature range is explored (room temp through ~700°C)?
- **Sputtering parameters:** Record power, pressure, Ar flow rate, and target-to-substrate distance for each layer. Are Nb and Sn layers deposited in the same chamber without breaking vacuum?
- **Effect of temperature on sticking coefficient and adatom mobility:** The paper should discuss how higher substrate temperature promotes diffusion and phase formation in-situ. Understand the physics.
- **Comparison to post-deposition anneal approach:** Does in-situ heating during deposition produce better results than room-temperature deposition followed by anneal? Note the trade-offs.

# 🧪 Paper 7 — Sayeed et al. — Co-Sputtering (ODU/Jefferson Lab)

**Citation:** Md Nizam Sayeed et al., Old Dominion University / Jefferson Lab

**Title:** Fabrication of Superconducting Nb₃Sn Film by Co-Sputtering

## 👤 Kegan — Characterization Focus

- **Compositional control via co-sputtering:** Co-sputtering allows continuous tuning of Nb:Sn ratio by adjusting relative power. How closely does the deposited composition match the target 3:1 Nb:Sn ratio?
- **Phase map as a function of power ratio:** Is there an XRD phase map showing which phases (Nb₃Sn, NbSn₂, Nb₆Sn₅) appear at different Nb/Sn power ratios?
- **Grain size and morphology:** Compare grain size to the multilayer approach (Paper 6). Does co-sputtering produce finer or coarser grains?
- **Tc and transition width:** Record best Tc and ΔTc. Is the transition as sharp as multilayer or bronze-route approaches?
- **Impurity content:** Co-sputtering from elemental targets should give low impurity films. Does the paper report any impurity measurements (SIMS, RBS)?

## ⚙️ Andrea Q — Deposition Focus

- **Dual-target configuration:** Describe the geometry — two magnetron guns (Nb + Sn) firing simultaneously. What are the individual power levels for the stoichiometric composition?
- **Challenges of co-sputtering Nb and Sn:** Nb and Sn have very different sputter yields and melting points. How does the group address Sn re-evaporation at elevated substrate temperatures?
- **Composition vs. power calibration:** How is the Nb:Sn ratio calibrated (XRF, RBS, EDS on witness samples)? What is the precision of compositional control?
- **Deposition rate and uniformity:** What total deposition rate is achieved? Is the film composition uniform across the substrate?
- **In-situ vs. ex-situ phase formation:** At what substrate temperature does the A15 phase form during co-sputtering? Is a post-deposition anneal needed?

# 🧪 Paper 8 — Sayeed et al. — Single Target (ODU/Jefferson Lab)

**Citation:** Md Nizam Sayeed et al., Old Dominion University / Jefferson Lab

**Title:** Properties of Nb₃Sn Films Fabricated by Magnetron Sputtering from a Single Target

## 👤 Kegan — Characterization Focus

- **Composition drift over time:** A compound Nb₃Sn target loses Sn preferentially due to its lower surface binding energy. Track any reported time-dependent composition shift in deposited films (early vs. late in target lifetime).
- **Phase purity and XRD:** Does a single-target approach maintain stoichiometric Nb₃Sn, or is there evidence of off-stoichiometry phases? Compare FWHM to Papers 6 and 7.
- **Tc and ΔTc:** Record the best values and compare across all three Sayeed papers. Is single-target, co-sputtering, or multilayer best for transition sharpness?
- **SEM surface morphology:** Note grain size, surface roughness, and any pit or void formation. Rough surfaces increase Rs.
- **RRR or resistivity:** If normal-state resistivity is reported, it provides a proxy for impurity/disorder level.

## ⚙️ Andrea Q — Deposition Focus

- **Single compound target preparation:** How is the stoichiometric Nb₃Sn target made? Sintering? Hot pressing? What is the reported target density?
- **Preferential sputtering management:** This is the key challenge. Does the group use any pre-sputtering conditioning protocol? How long does it take to reach steady-state composition?
- **Power and pressure optimization:** Record the RF or DC power, Ar pressure, and substrate temperature used. How do these compare to co-sputtering (Paper 7)?
- **Film thickness series:** What thicknesses are investigated? Is there a minimum thickness needed for phase-pure Nb₃Sn?
- **Scalability assessment:** A single compound target is simpler to operate than dual targets but harder to maintain stoichiometry. What does this paper conclude about scalability?
